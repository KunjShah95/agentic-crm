# Estate360 — Product Demo Script

**Length:** 3:20 · **Format:** screen capture with VO, no stock footage
**Captions:** burned-in, quiet rail register
**Tone:** the voice of someone who has run a sales floor and is tired of spreadsheets

---

## Direction

The whole video is one continuous camera move through one workspace. No logo
stinger, no swipe transition, no stock footage of people in glass offices. The
product is the only thing on screen, and the camera behaves like a person leaning
over a colleague's shoulder.

The rule that keeps it from feeling like a demo reel: **every claim on screen is
a thing the software just did.** No dashboards populated with invented metrics,
no fake testimonials, no "trusted by teams across India" with logos that do not
exist. Where a number appears, it is a real record from the seeded workspace, and
the VO says what it is rather than how impressive it is.

---

## Shot list

### 1 · Open on the morning brief — 0:00–0:22

**Frame:** Browser at `/{workspace}/dashboard`. The page has just loaded. Skeleton
rails are still resolving in the stat band; the Next Best Action panel is already
populated.

**VO:**
> It's nine in the morning. Rahul Shah opened the same cost sheet three times
> yesterday and nobody called him back. Estate360 opens with that as the first
> row.

**Notes:** Hold two beats on the load state. The skeleton is not filler here — it
is the point. The product is doing work before a human has touched anything.

---

### 2 · The ranked queue — 0:22–0:52

**Frame:** Cursor moves down the action list. Hover-revealed row actions fade in
on the row under the cursor. Camera pushes in slightly on the first row.

**VO:**
> Twelve new enquiries overnight. They're already sorted. Hot leads first,
> because the hot ones cool off in about forty minutes. Priya's site visit is
> tomorrow, so the cost sheet goes out today. A payment milestone on A-1204 went
> overdue yesterday, and that's already queued for the accounts team.

**Notes:** This is the only shot where a count appears on screen. It comes from
the seeded workspace, and the VO says "twelve" because that is what is there.

---

### 3 · One command, everything finds it — 0:52–1:18

**Frame:** ⌘K. Type `shah`. Results resolve in three groups. Arrow down once, hit
enter.

**VO:**
> Everything's connected. Search a name and you get the contact, their deals,
> and every conversation with them. Same place, one keystroke.

**Notes:** The command palette is the cheapest thing in the video to film and the
most persuasive. The in-flight search dims the previous results rather than
blanking them — show that if the timing works, because it is a small detail that
signals care.

---

### 4 · The deal moves, the paperwork writes itself — 1:18–2:02

**Frame:** Kanban board. Drag a deal from Qualified to Booking. The card settles.
Cut to the deal detail. The activity timeline has gained an entry. Scroll to
Milestones — eight rows, populated.

**VO:**
> Booking confirmed. That one drag opened eight payment milestones and the first
> demand letter. Nobody typed the schedule in. Nobody will forget to send the
> third one, because it's already sitting there waiting for its date.

**Notes:** The money shot. The drag is slow enough to follow. The cut to the
timeline is motivated — it is the consequence of the drag, shown immediately, not
saved for a montage.

---

### 5 · The cost sheet that argues with nobody — 2:02–2:34

**Frame:** Unit detail. Base price, GST, stamp duty, other charges, total. Copy
the total. Paste it into the WhatsApp thread, which opens beside it with the
buyer already in Gujarati.

**VO:**
> The cost sheet and the demand letter read from the same numbers. So when the
> buyer says the price you quoted was different, you don't go looking for the
> spreadsheet that was right. The invoice is the record.

**Notes:** End on the WhatsApp thread in Gujarati. It is the shot that says "this
was built here" without a single word of narration about India.

---

### 6 · Broker sees their units, not yours — 2:34–2:56

**Frame:** Switch to a channel-partner view. The inventory list shows six units.
The full project has ninety-one.

**VO:**
> Your broker logs in and sees the six units you allocated to them. Not the other
> eighty-five. The commission math is already done, so the argument about who
> showed that buyer never happens in the first place.

**Notes:** Brief. The point lands in three seconds; do not over-explain it.

---

### 7 · Close — 2:56–3:20

**Frame:** Pull back to the dashboard, then out. Title card on the warm canvas:
**"Possession is not luck."** One line of body copy, one button.

**VO:**
> Estate360. Fourteen days, no card, and your data stays yours — export it
> whenever you want.

## Craft notes

**Captions.** Burned in, two lines maximum, positioned on the lower third with a
scrim only where they overlap content. Never over the UI row that is being
pointed at — the viewer has to be able to read the interface and the words at the
same time, and the interface is the proof.

**Pacing.** One cut every 8–14 seconds. Each shot has exactly one idea. If a shot
needs two, it is two shots.

**Sound.** No music bed under the first four shots — the VO carries it. Music
enters at 2:34, one beat before the broker shot, and resolves on the title card.

**No fake numbers, no invented people.** This constraint is not a stylistic
preference. Fabricated metrics and testimonials are the fastest way to lose a
deal in a face-to-face sale, because the prospect will check. Every figure in this
video comes from the seeded workspace.

**No feature laundry list.** Nine capabilities exist in the product. This video
shows six. The three it skips are the ones that need a screen each to explain and
a paragraph of copy to land.
